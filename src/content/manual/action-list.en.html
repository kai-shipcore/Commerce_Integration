Action List

Combine forecast demand, available inventory, preorder backlog, and confirmed inbound stock to find the SKUs that need attention now and review their recommended order quantities.

💡
What this page is for: It turns the SKU population into a prioritized worklist based on demand and inventory risk. A recommended order is a starting point for planner review, not an automatic purchase order or final approval.

The easiest workflow

1

Check the data and model

Start with Trained through, Model, Horizon to, and Forecast Server status. If the forecast is stale, open Run forecast to refresh it.

2

Set the planning assumptions

Match lead time, review period, service level, and risk window to the current supply conditions. Changing a value asks the server to recalculate the list and recommendations.

3

Choose a worklist card

Narrow the list with Preorder, No stock on hand, Best seller, Out within N days, Dry before inbound, Already drafted, or Routine. Click the active card again to return to All.

4

Check the evidence

Do not read the order quantity alone. Check available stock, inbound, stockout timing, and reliability. Open a SKU to see the arithmetic, sales and forecast charts, and backtest evidence.

5

Export a working list

Apply the filters and sort you need, then select Export CSV. The export contains all rows in the filtered view.

Run forecast

When Trained through is stale or new orders must be included, expand the Run forecast section.

ElementHow to use it
HorizonChoose 13, 26, or 52 weeks. A longer horizon produces forecasts farther into the future.
Pipeline stagesThe run proceeds through Sync → Ingest → Profile → Forecast. Sync commonly takes several minutes.
Screens updatedA successful run refreshes Action List and Forecast Validation. SKU Planning continues using the weekly scheduled forecast.
Cannot be interruptedThe panel deliberately has no Stop button while running. Interrupting between sales, profile, and forecast writes could leave their reference weeks inconsistent, so wait for completion.
Failure or external cancellationA failure leaves the previous forecast in place. If an external cancellation warning appears, rerun to completion because the three datasets may describe different weeks.
Run logExpand the log after completion or failure when you need the stage counts and warnings.

Forecast and Not forecast

Forecast

These SKUs have a weekly demand forecast. Forecast demand and error are combined with stock, preorder backlog, and inbound supply to calculate stockout timing and a recommended order.

Not forecast

These SKUs sell too irregularly for a weekly forecast. The page shows cover based on the last 13 weeks of actual sales, but does not invent a model-based order recommendation or reliability score.

Forecast summary cards

Each card is both a count and a filter.

CardWhat it shows
AllAll currently forecastable SKUs. Use it to return from another worklist card.
PreorderSKUs that require priority attention because they carry preorder backlog.
No stock on handEvery SKU whose available inventory is at or below zero. This can differ from the row's No Stock priority: a SKU with both no stock and backlog is badged Preorder but still belongs to this card.
Best sellerThe smallest group of SKUs that together contributes half of trailing four-week demand. It represents product importance, not inventory risk, so its star can appear on Preorder, No Stock, or Routine rows.
Out ≤N daysSKUs expected to exhaust current stock inside the selected Risk window.
Dry before inboundSKUs that have confirmed inbound supply but are expected to run out before it lands. Expediting or reallocating existing stock may be faster than placing another order.
Already draftedSKUs with units on a draft container. Use it to review items that may already be in progress. Draft units are not committed inbound and are not subtracted from the recommendation.
RoutineSKUs with neither preorder backlog nor a no-stock condition, reviewed on the normal ordering cycle.
Units rec.The recommendation total for the SKUs currently left on screen. When narrowed it reads units rec. · this view and shows the full-list total underneath.

Planning assumptions

SettingPlain-language meaningRange / default
Lead time (weeks)How long a new order takes to become available. A longer lead time usually means protecting more future demand and can increase the recommendation.1–52 / 8 weeks
Review (weeks)How long inventory must last after the order arrives until the next order review. If ordering is reviewed weekly, use 1.1–13 / 1 week
Service levelHow much protection to hold against forecast error. Choose 84%, 90%, 95%, or 98%. A higher level can increase safety stock and the recommendation.84% / default
Risk window (days)The horizon used by the “Out ≤N days” focus card. It is a review filter, separate from the order-coverage period.1–365 / 30 days
🔗
Assumptions carry into detail. When you open a SKU, the current assumptions are included in its URL. The list and detail therefore answer under the same conditions, and a shared link reproduces those conditions.

Filters, columns, sorting, and export

  • Search: Enter any part of a SKU or product name.
  • Category: Keep one product category.
  • Reliability: Filter to good, fair, poor, or not measured.
  • Demand trend: Filter to rising, steady, falling, collapsing, or unknown. It compares the latest four weeks with the seasonally adjusted trailing-12-week average.
  • Data-quality warnings: A line above the table shows the number of flagged SKUs and each warning type. Select the overall warning count or a specific warning label to filter, and select it again to clear.
  • Rows: Show 25, 50, 100, 200, or all rows. The table scrolls vertically and horizontally while the headers and SKU column remain pinned.
  • Columns: Show only the Position, Demand, or Action columns needed for the task. Toggle an individual column or a whole group. SKU and Priority remain visible, and at least one optional column must remain.
  • Saved column preference: The selection is stored in the current browser and restored on later visits. “N hidden” means columns were previously hidden. Use Show every column to restore them.
  • Sort: Click a column heading to sort. Click again to reverse it and a third time to return to worklist order. Shift-click another heading to add a secondary sort.
  • Reset: Clears the worklist card, search, category, reliability, demand trend, quality warning, and manual sorting. It does not reset planning assumptions or saved columns.
  • Export CSV: Downloads all rows in the current filtered and sorted view, not only the visible page.

Portfolio demand chart

“Demand across these N SKUs” totals the last 26 weeks of actual sales and the forward forecast for the SKUs left by the current filters. Changing a filter also changes the chart population. How to read the Forecast table

The table reads from Position → Demand → Action. SKU and Priority are fixed; use Columns to show or hide the remaining columns.

ColumnMeaning
SKUThe Master SKU. Category and product name appear below it; long names are truncated and available on hover. A warning triangle indicates a data-quality flag.
PriorityThe stock situation: Preorder, No Stock, or Routine. When conditions overlap, the first match wins in that order. The ★ beside it is not another priority; it marks a product in the group generating half of four-week demand.
AvailableCurrent available inventory used by the planning calculation.
Preord.Preorder backlog still owed to customers.
InboundConfirmed inbound units. “In N days” shows time to the earliest inbound; hover for its ETA date.
4wkActual units sold in the latest four weekly buckets, approximately 28 days.
TrendLatest four weeks divided by the trailing-12-week average: rising >1.25, steady 0.80–1.25, falling 0.40–0.80, collapsing <0.40, or a dash when history is insufficient.
Next NwForecast demand over lead time + reorder cycle. Changing the assumptions changes both N and this quantity. It is the demand directly used by the recommendation.
Stocks outEstimated days until current stock is exhausted. Values inside 14 days are emphasized in red. If stock runs dry before inbound lands, the inbound timing is shown too.
OrderThe recommended quantity under the current assumptions. “N drafted” underneath means that many units sit on a draft container; because they are not committed, they are not subtracted.
ReliabilityForecast error measured by backtesting: ●●● good (≤15%), ●●○ fair (15–30%), ●○○ poor (>30%), or ○○○ not measured.

SKU detail

Select a SKU in the Forecast table to open its detail. Use Previous, Next, or the SKU selector to move through the worklist order.

AreaWhat to check
Supply-gap warningShows the gap between stock exhaustion and confirmed inbound arrival. If a new order cannot arrive sooner, the page directs attention to expediting or reallocating rather than buying more.
Recommended orderShows the calculation line by line: demand owed, expected sales, and an error cushion are added; available stock and qualifying inbound are subtracted. It also shows a plausible range and explains when the service level pushes the recommendation above it.
Forecast reliabilityShows average backtest error and predicted-versus-actual results by window. If the SKU has no measured error, the substitute basis—such as a promoted-SKU cohort or segment median—is named.
Weekly demandCompares actual sales and model forecast over time. A separate warning may appear when the model runs materially above the recent selling rate.
Weekly figuresExpand to inspect model forecast and Spreadsheet V1 week by week. A dash means V1 does not cover that week; it does not mean zero.
How the forecast was testedShows how the model performed on historical 10-week periods it had not seen when producing each test forecast.

How to read Not forecast

Not-forecast SKUs use the last 13 weeks of actual sales. Filter with Selling, Dormant, Runs out in lead time, or No stock, and use SKU/product search, category, sorting, pagination, and CSV export.

ColumnMeaning
13w demandActual sales over the trailing 13 weeks, not a forecast.
Per weekThe weekly selling rate implied by those actual sales.
Last saleThe most recent week with recorded sales.
Available / InboundCurrent available inventory and confirmed inbound. A dash may mean there is no record or that the value does not apply; it is not automatically zero.
CoverEstimated days of cover if the recent selling rate continues. It is highlighted with a warning when shorter than lead time.
⚠️
Not-forecast SKUs have no recommended order. This is intentional, not missing output: the application avoids applying a demand model to irregular sales. Review recent actual sales, open orders, MOQ, supply constraints, and planner judgment instead.

Checklist before approving an order

  • Confirm that inventory is not sample data and reflects the latest synchronization.
  • Confirm inbound quantities and ETAs against the real PO or container records.
  • Apply MOQ, order multiples, container capacity, supplier closures, and the real lead time.
  • For poor or unmeasured reliability, separately review recent sales, promotions, seasonality, and discontinuation plans.
  • If stock runs dry before inbound and a new order would arrive even later, review expediting, emergency freight, or stock reallocation before increasing the order.

Troubleshooting

SymptomWhat to check
The list cannot loadCheck Forecast Server status and confirm that the FastAPI service configured by AI_SERVICE_URL is running, then retry.
Loading continues after a changeEach planning-setting change starts a server recalculation. Avoid changing several values rapidly and wait for the last request. If it remains stuck, review server logs and any 502, 503, or 504 error record.
List and detail quantities differConfirm that lead time, review period, service level, and risk window in the detail URL match those used in the list.
No rows matchSelect Reset to clear the focus card and filters, and confirm whether you are viewing Forecast or Not forecast.
A dash appears instead of zero stockIn Not forecast, a dash means no inventory record; zero means the record explicitly reports no stock.
