Forecast Validation

Evidence showing whether the current model improves on Spreadsheet V1, whether the improvement is broad across SKUs, and whether accuracy holds up in live operation.

💡
How this differs from Action List: Action List asks “What should we review and order?” Forecast Validation asks “How much evidence have we earned for trusting the forecast behind that decision?” These figures are model adoption and monitoring evidence, not purchase instructions.

Recommended review order

1

Check provenance

Confirm Forecast Server status and review Model, Evaluated on, Scored, and Forecast trained through.

2

Read performance and scope together

Compare WAPE in Model versus spreadsheet, then use How demand is shaped to see how much of the catalogue and demand that claim covers.

3

See when and how errors happened

Use Demand vs forecast to compare actual and predicted lines by lead and inspect over- or under-forecasting over time.

4

Review live evidence

Check how forecasts issued before outcomes were known scored as their weeks completed.

5

Test the breadth of improvement

In SKU-level breakdown, set a minimum volume and compare win rate by SKU, win rate by demand, regressions, and every scored row.

6

Preserve the independent test

Confirm the quarantined final window has not been spent during development.

Key terms

TermPlain-language meaning
Spreadsheet / V1The previous forecasting method used as the baseline, scored on the same SKUs and weeks.
SegmentA group of SKUs with similar demand behavior. A model can win overall while losing in one segment.
Backtest windowA historical test where the model sees only information available at a past cutoff and forecasts what happened afterward.
SKU-window rowOne SKU scored on one backtest window. A SKU tested on several windows contributes several rows.
Served forecastA forecast stored and issued before its outcome was known. It becomes scorable only after the target week finishes.
Units soldActual product quantity sold in a scored window—not an error rate.
Percentage points (pp)The direct difference between two percentage rates. For example, 40% − 25% = 15pp. It is not a unit quantity.

Understanding WAPE and bias

WAPE (Weighted Absolute Percentage Error) expresses total forecast miss as a share of actual demand. Lower is better.

WAPE = sum of absolute errors ÷ sum of actual units × 100

1,000 actual units and 180 units of absolute error → WAPE 18%

Difference = Model WAPE − V1 WAPE. Negative is improvement; positive is regression.

  • Pooled WAPE gives high-demand SKUs more influence. A good pooled figure does not mean every SKU won.
  • WAPE removes direction. Positive Bias means over-forecasting, negative means under-forecasting, and near zero is more balanced.
  • Over-forecasting leaves inventory; under-forecasting loses sales. Equal-sized bias can therefore have different operating costs.

4. Performance on forecasts actually served

Scores forecasts that were issued before outcomes were known, using only weeks that have completed. Once enough history accumulates, this is stronger evidence than a development backtest.

  • Runs are newest first, with run date, weeks scored, overall WAPE, bias, and SKU count.
  • Select a row to open per-segment WAPE, bias, SKU count, and actual units below.
  • One or two scored weeks are shallow evidence; the screen warns when that is the case.
  • Rows from another model version may be sample data and must be excluded according to the displayed warning.
  • No runs or no completed weeks is a valid early state. The first score appears at least one week after the first stored run.

5. SKU-level breakdown

This replaces the old “five best and five worst” lists. It tests whether improvement is broad or whether a few large wins carry the average.

Set the scope

FilterHow to use it
Minimum units soldKeep rows with at least 0, 100, 200, 300, or 500 actual units in a scored window. Raising it reduces percentage swings caused by tiny denominators and focuses on material demand.
WindowSelect All or one backtest window.
SegmentSelect All or one demand segment.
Scope statementReports remaining SKU-window rows, units sold, share of all scored demand, and excluded row count. Keep this statement with any reported result.

Four headline measures

MeasureMeaning
Win rate, by SKUShare of selected SKU-window rows where model WAPE is below V1.
Win rate, by demandShare of selected actual units sitting on rows the model wins. Above the SKU rate means wins are concentrated in larger-volume rows.
Pooled WAPEModel and V1 demand-weighted error recalculated over the current scope.
Material regressionsRows where model WAPE is at least 25pp worse than V1, plus their share of units sold.

Distribution and detail

  • Distribution of per-SKU differences: x-axis is Model WAPE − V1 WAPE. Negative/left is improvement; positive/right is regression. Units are percentage points, not products sold.
  • By window and by segment: compare row count, win rate, model pooled WAPE, and V1 WAPE. A group can lose on row count but win on large-volume error, so read both measures.
  • Every scored SKU-window: search and sort every row by SKU, units sold, either WAPE, or difference. The table starts with 50 and Show more adds 50.
  • Difference sign: negative/green means the model is closer; positive/amber means V1 is closer.
  • SKU link: opens Action List detail. Investigate rows combining high volume, large regression, and a large recommendation first.

6. Final test window

A period quarantined throughout development. Repeatedly viewing and tuning against it would introduce optimism, so it is reserved for one adoption decision after development.

  • Not evaluated yet: the independent test is intentionally preserved; this is not an error.
  • Results not rendered yet: evaluation has run but the result view is not ready.
  • When available, compare it in the same form as section 1 and check agreement with development backtests and live performance.

Decision examples

  • Positive: pooled WAPE beats V1, both win rates are high, and key segments, live runs, and the final test agree.
  • Improvement carried by large SKUs: SKU win rate is low but demand win rate and pooled WAPE are good. This can help the portfolio while losing SKUs still need manual review.
  • Improvement carried by small SKUs: SKU win rate is high but demand win rate is low. Many rows win, but V1 may remain better on core volume.
  • Insufficient evidence: too few eligible SKUs, too little scored demand, or too few completed live weeks.
  • Reconsider: material regressions carry high volume and live WAPE, bias, and the final test also deteriorate.
⚠️
Interpret with care. Backtests describe historical conditions and cannot guarantee promotions, price changes, stockouts, launches, discontinuations, or market shifts. Never report a favorable filtered subset as overall performance; include its remaining row count and share of scored demand.

No predicted line or live performanceConfirm that real forecast runs are stored and at least one target sales week has completed.
Sample-data warningExclude those rows or lines from accuracy decisions and wait for real current-model runs.
No SKU rows matchReset Minimum units sold, Window, and Segment to widen the scope.
