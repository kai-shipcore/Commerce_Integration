OOS Impact Analysis

A simple view of how much sales fall during a stockout and how quickly they recover after restocking.

🚧
This page is currently a UI design prototype. The SKUs, sales figures, and percentages such as 32% are sample data for reviewing the screen. The page is not connected to the production database yet and must not be interpreted as actual company performance.

What this screen shows

① How much do sales fall after switching to Pre-Order?

Compare Shopify sales before and after a product changes from normal sales to Pre-Order. A large drop may mean that customers do not want to wait.

② How quickly do sales return after restocking?

Measure how much of the pre-stockout sales level returns after 30, 60, and 90 days on Amazon, eBay, and Walmart.

💼
Business purpose: A stockout may hurt more than the days with no inventory. Orders may fall during Pre-Order, and marketplace ranking or visibility may take time to return after restocking. This design helps support decisions about purchase timing, safety stock, and which SKUs to replenish first.

Simple glossary

TermPlain-language meaning
Normal Daily AverageThe average number of units sold per day before the product switched to Pre-Order.
PO Daily AverageThe average number of units ordered per day after the product switched to Pre-Order.
Drop RateThe percentage by which sales slowed versus normal sales. A larger value means a larger impact.
BaselineThe normal sales speed before the stockout, used as the comparison target.
Recovery RateHow much of the pre-stockout sales speed has returned. A value of 100% means sales are back to the old level.

Drop-rate formula

Drop rate = (Normal daily average − PO daily average) ÷ Normal daily average × 100

Example: normal sales are 10 units/day and Pre-Order sales are 6 units/day. The drop is (10 − 6) ÷ 10 × 100 = 40%.

Summary cardMeaning
Analyzed SKUsThe number of products matching the selected filters.
Average Sales DropAdd every SKU's drop rate and divide by the number of SKUs. It shows the overall impact.
Median DropSort the drop rates and take the middle. It is less affected by unusually high or low values than the average.
Largest Drop SKUThe product with the largest drop and a priority candidate for investigation.

Understanding the histogram and the 32% median

Each bar shows how many SKUs fall inside a percentage range. The dashed line is not the average of one bar; it is the median of all selected SKUs.

🧮
The ten sample drop rates sorted from low to high are 10, 18, 21, 22, 29, 35, 50, 50, 64, 74. With ten values, the median is the average of the 5th and 6th values: (29 + 35) ÷ 2 = 32%. This means half of the selected SKUs are at or below 32%, and half are at or above 32%.
ℹ️
The median does not delete or exclude outliers. It is simply less sensitive to extreme values than the average. Use the average for the overall impact and the median for a more typical SKU.

Screen 2 — Marketplace Restock Recovery

This screen answers, “Do sales return immediately after inventory returns?” It focuses on Amazon, eBay, and Walmart, where a stockout may affect search visibility and sales ranking.

🏃
If a product sold 10 units/day before the stockout, that is the 100% baseline. Selling 5/day after restocking means 50% recovery, 8/day means 80%, and returning to 10/day means 100% recovery.

Recovery formula

Recovery rate = Post-restock daily average ÷ Pre-stockout baseline × 100

Example: the baseline is 10 units/day and the post-restock average is 7/day. Recovery is 7 ÷ 10 × 100 = 70%.

Decisions this analysis is intended to support

  • Replenishment priority: replenish SKUs with a large Pre-Order drop and slow restock recovery first.
  • Safety stock: hold more protection inventory for products that suffer more from stockouts.
  • Channel follow-up: consider advertising, price, and listing checks when a marketplace recovers slowly.
  • Acceptable OOS duration: compare short and long stockouts to decide how much stockout risk the business can tolerate.

Important interpretation limits

CautionWhy it matters
Not every drop is caused by OOSPrice, ads, seasonality, competition, reviews, and promotions can also change sales.
Small samples are unstableWhen only a few SKUs are selected, one unusual SKU can move the average considerably.
Compare equal-length windowsComparing 30 days before with only 5 days after is not a fair comparison.
Check zero baselinesA product with no pre-stockout sales cannot produce a meaningful drop or recovery rate.
Correlation is not proof of causeA drop after Pre-Order does not prove that Pre-Order was the only cause.
