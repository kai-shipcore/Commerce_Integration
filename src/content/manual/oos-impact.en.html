OOS Impact Analysis

Use actual inventory and sales history to understand how stockouts changed demand and whether sales returned to their former level after restocking.

💡
The page in one sentence: the first tab asks “How much did Shopify demand fall or rise after conversion to Pre-Order?” and the second asks “How many months did marketplace sales take to recover after restocking?”

Recommended first-time workflow

1

Check the data timestamp

Review the Synced timestamp in the upper-right corner. If fresher data is required and you have edit permission, click Sync. Both tabs reload after a successful sync.

2

Choose the tab that matches your question

Use Shopify · Pre-Order Conversion Drop for demand during stockout, or Marketplaces · Restock Recovery for post-restock marketplace performance.

3

Narrow the scope

Select product categories, Shopify channels, stage, or the Top 100 seller condition. Clicking a distribution bar filters the detail table to the same range.

4

Move from summary to evidence

Start with KPIs and distributions, then click a Master SKU row. The detail panel shows the quantities, dates, baseline, and actual sales trajectory behind the result.

1. Shopify · Pre-Order Conversion Drop

Shopify can continue accepting Pre-Orders while an item is unavailable. The tab therefore compares normal demand immediately before stockout with Pre-Order demand after stockout over equal-length windows.

Filters

FilterDescription
ItemMulti-select Car Cover, Seat Cover, Floor Mat, and SWC.
ChannelMulti-select Coverland B2C, Coverland B2B, and Icarcover. Amazon, eBay, and Walmart do not apply because this tab measures Shopify Pre-Orders.
StatusPre-Order Active is a preliminary result that can change until restock. Restocked is a finalized Pre-Order window.

How the comparison window is chosen

  • The inventory-history stockout start date is the Pre-Order conversion anchor.
  • The normal window ends immediately before the anchor; the Pre-Order window starts on the anchor.
  • The before and after windows always have equal length and are capped at 30 days.
  • If restock occurred sooner, the actual Pre-Order duration is used on both sides. Example: restocked after 18 days → compare 18 days before with 18 days after.
  • Active episodes are preliminary because the available window can still grow. Only the latest settled data window is used to avoid very recent data lag.

Drop-rate formula

Sales drop rate = (Normal daily average − Pre-Order daily average) ÷ Normal daily average × 100

Example 1: 10 units/day normally → 6 units/day on Pre-Order = 40% drop

Example 2: 1.28 units/day normally → 2.06 units/day on Pre-Order = -61% drop, or a 61% sales increase

📈
A negative result is not an error. It means demand increased after conversion to Pre-Order. The screen uses a blue Sales increase badge. Strong demand while unavailable may justify higher replenishment priority.

Reading the KPI cards

KPIMeaningWhen clicked

Restocked Average · FinalThe average drop among reliable, completed episodes. The record count and median appear below it.Filters to restocked episodes and updates the Status filter.

Click the active KPI again to clear its filter. The median on the histogram represents the middle episode and is less affected by one extreme value; the calculation explanation is shown under the chart.

🔎
KPI and detail counts may differ. KPIs and the distribution use only reliable episodes. Without a KPI filter, the detail table keeps short raw episodes for verification. One SKU may also appear in multiple channels or stockout episodes, so a row is not always a unique SKU.

2. Marketplaces · Restock Recovery

This tab combines Amazon, Walmart, and eBay-related marketplace sales to determine whether a previously unavailable Master SKU returned to its pre-stockout sales level. It shows one row per Master SKU, using only its latest stockout episode, rather than one row per channel.

ℹ️
Eligible SKUs: the latest stockout episode must be restocked and at least 7 days old. A SKU that is currently out of stock again is excluded because recovery cannot be measured. Near-zero baselines that would display as 0.0 are also excluded to reduce noise.

Filters and KPIs

ElementDescription
All Channels CombinedRestock is one physical inventory event, so marketplace demand is summed and evaluated per Master SKU.
Top 100 Sellers OnlyKeeps the top 100 Master SKUs by trailing-30-day sales across all channels, including Shopify. Use it to review core products first.
ItemMulti-select Car Cover, Seat Cover, Floor Mat, and SWC.
SKUs TrackedMaster SKU count remaining under the current filters.
Avg. Days to RecoveryThe average of confirmed 30-, 60-, and 90-day classifications. It is not an average of exact daily crossing dates.
Not RecoveredSKUs older than 90 days whose Month 1, 2, and 3 averages all missed the threshold.
PendingSKUs that have not reached the threshold but are not yet 90 days past restock.

Recovery calculation

ValueDefinition
Pre-stockout baselineCombined marketplace daily average during the 30 days immediately before stockout.
Month 1 recoveryDays 0–30 average ÷ baseline × 100. It remains — until the full 30 days have elapsed.
Month 2 recoveryDays 30–60 average ÷ baseline × 100. It remains — until 60 days have elapsed.
Month 3 recoveryDays 60–90 average ÷ baseline × 100. It remains — until 90 days have elapsed.
Recovery thresholdDefaults to 90%. Enter an integer from 50 to 100, then press Enter or leave the field to recalculate.
Days to RecoveryDisplays 30, 60, or 90 according to the first completed month that reaches the threshold. It is not the exact day of crossing.

Example: baseline 10 units/day, threshold 90%

Month 1: 7 units/day = 70% → not recovered

Month 2: 9.5 units/day = 95% → Slow Recovery at 60 days

Status definitions

StatusMeaning
Normal RecoveryMonth 1 reached the threshold, producing a 30-day result.
Slow RecoveryMonth 2 or Month 3 first reached the threshold, producing a 60- or 90-day result.
PendingNo completed month has reached the threshold, but fewer than 90 days have elapsed.
Not RecoveredAll three month blocks missed the threshold after 90 days.

Distribution, table, and detail chart

  • Recovery distribution: groups results into 0–30 days, 30–60 days, 60–90 days, Pending, and Not Recovered. Click a bar to filter the table.
  • Detail table: compare stockout date, restock date, stockout duration, monthly recovery rates, and status. The default is 10 rows; options are 10, 25, 50, and 100.
  • Trailing 14-Day Avg.: a smoothed, contextual view of actual sales. It may plot only dates with sales. Crossing the baseline on this line does not determine recovery.
  • Daily Sales: actual day-level quantity. It can be volatile because of order bursts and zero-sale days, so use it to inspect the raw pattern.
  • Recovery marker: the 30-, 60-, or 90-day marker corresponds to the monthly classification in the table. Recovery is always judged from the same monthly averages.

Operational examples

  • Purchase priority: review SKUs with the largest daily Pre-Order loss or stronger demand while unavailable.
  • Protect core products: enable Top 100 Sellers Only and review Slow Recovery or Not Recovered products for safety stock and lead-time changes.
  • Marketplace follow-up: for Not Recovered products, check price, ads, search visibility, reviews, competitors, and listing health—not inventory alone.
  • Scenario comparison: compare thresholds such as 80%, 90%, and 100% to distinguish minimum recovery from full recovery.
⚠️
Interpret with care. This page shows changes associated with stockout timing; it does not prove stockout or Pre-Order was the only cause. Review price, promotions, advertising, seasonality, competition, listing visibility, and channel-policy changes before making a final decision.
